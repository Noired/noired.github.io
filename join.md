# Join the group

In January 2027 I will start my research group at the [CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security](https://cispa.de/) in St. Ingbert, near Saarbrücken, Germany. I am looking for a PhD student and a postdoc to join me as early as March 2027. Applications are open now; I am planning to start interviews in November.

## What will we work on?

A line of research I have been working on, and will keep expanding, applies the principles of Geometric Deep Learning to the study of Large Language Models (LLMs), and neural networks more generally. I see these models, and the "artefacts" they produce (such as activations, attention maps, gradients and output probability distributions), as complex *structured* data modalities in their own right. The idea is to design structure-aware methods that can efficiently learn how the computation of LLMs is structured, represent how they behave, tell whether different models work alike, predict when they fail, and work out how they can be steered toward safer and better-aligned generations.

You can see this area as sitting at the crossroads of [Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16496) and [Representation Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19649) on one hand, and [Weight Space Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10090) and, as already mentioned, [Geometric Deep Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13478) on the other. Against this backdrop, auditing, monitoring, and broader problems in AI Safety, as well as parameter-efficient finetuning and model merging, are natural applications.

For a more concrete idea, have a look at my [recent papers](/works) and at [this blog post](https://ffabffrasca.substack.com/p/detecting-llm-misbehaviors-from-the), where I bring together a series of works on learning from model internals for hallucination and data contamination detection.

You can also get a sense of the area by browsing the website of ["Neural Network Artifacts as a New Data Modality"](https://artifactsasdata.org/), a NeurIPS 2026 workshop I am co-organising. If you are in Paris for NeurIPS, come and say hi on 13 December 2026 at the Palais des Congrès, room 241.

Examples of high-level questions I find exciting right now:

- How should we represent language models and their internal computations? What structure and symmetries do these have, and how should architectures respect them?
- Can we learn representations that help us understand how models work and how they relate to each other? Can we transfer skills and interpretability insights across models?
- Can we build accurate and efficient structure-aware monitors that detect failure modes of LLMs and agentic systems, such as hallucinations, sycophancy, deception and jailbreaks? When does modelling structure actually help compared with simple probes, and does it generalise better across models, domains, tasks and attacks?
- How can structure guide mitigation? Can we design structure-aware steering approaches that correct a model's behaviour with minimal side effects on its capabilities?

Beyond the above, I keep an active interest in learning on graphs and structured data more generally, including sets, sequences, point clouds, higher-order relational structures and combinations thereof. I am especially drawn to impactful applications where such data takes centre stage. One example is computational biology, where I am particularly interested in modelling RNA and chromatin structure.

All this gives a general idea of the directions I am currently exploring. The group's directions will also be shaped by the ideas and inclinations of its members, and by the work we do together as a team.

## Who am I looking for?

Above all, I am looking for <i>curious</i> people who like both careful thinking and getting things to work in practice. I aim to hire people who can work <i>independently</i> and also enjoy working <i>in a team</i>: people who are <i>responsible</i>, <i>open-minded</i> and <i>considerate</i> of others.

Technically speaking, the following are ***required***:
- Solid foundations in mathematics and machine learning
- Good coding skills and care for clean, well-structured code
- (**PhD student**) A Master's degree in computer science, mathematics, physics or a related field; completed or close to completion
- (**Postdoc**) A PhD in machine learning, AI or a related field (completed or close to completion), with a strong research record
- (**Postdoc**) The ability to drive research projects while working closely with the group, including mentoring students

While the following are ***nice to have***:
- Experience with any of: geometric deep learning, learning on graphs, mechanistic interpretability, AI safety, or the internals of language models more generally
- Familiarity with the concepts and mathematics of symmetries, equivariance, and graphs
- (**PhD student**) A peer-reviewed publication, or other research experience such as a research-oriented thesis
- (**Postdoc**) Ideas for a research direction of your own you would like to pursue

## What to expect?

**You will be joining a new lab.** As one of the first members of the group, you will get close one-to-one supervision early on, the chance to mentor interns and new students later, and the opportunity to help shape the lab's research direction and values.

**You will get a full-time, fully funded position at CISPA.** This comes with a competitive salary and the benefits of a German employment contract. You will also have access to CISPA's compute infrastructure and support for travelling to conferences.

**You will live and work in an international environment.** CISPA is a diverse, international workplace that operates fully in English. The group will be based at CISPA's site in St. Ingbert, a short commute from Saarbrücken, where I will be living myself. Saarbrücken is a university city close to the French border (Paris is under two hours away by train), home to Saarland University and several computer science research institutes — and the cost of living is moderate compared with larger German cities 😉.

You can read more about working at CISPA [here](https://cispa.de/en/career).

- Your up-to-date CV;

Formal applications go through CISPA’s application system, and I will point you to the next steps once we have been in touch.
